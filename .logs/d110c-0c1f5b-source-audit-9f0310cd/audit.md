## Concrete missing fact

The creator can currently certify only a dense prefix of author sequence numbers that it observed in the active close graph. A displaced source row consumes its author sequence permanently. Its genuine replacement receives a later sequence, while `completeRebaseSource()` records only the source row as locally published. Nothing delivered to the creator binds the source `(anchor, epoch, author, authorSequence, digest)` to the exact replacement digest(s) or to an authorized terminal disposition.

Consequently, with prior boundary `S`, displaced sources `S+1` and `S+2`, and replacements `S+3` and `S+4`, the close graph contains the replacements but not the sources. The adjacent-prefix loop remains at `S`. Using the maximum observed sequence would be unsafe: an unseen or substituted signed row at `S+1` would become covered without creator evidence. Keeping `S` is safe but permanently prevents later honest rows from becoming historical-covered after another close.

This same missing fact appears without rebase when `n+1` is admitted before `n`; after close/adoption, the old-anchor `n` can no longer enter the successor graph. The genesis foreign first-observed-above-one branch and a remove/re-add author whose prior aggregate entry was omitted are initial-state variants of the same proof problem.

6. `packages/node/src/v3-live.ts:6357-6481` enumerates displaced/historical rows; `readRebaseOutbox()` exposes source identity and application intents but no authenticated settlement proof. `completeRebaseSource()` at lines 6637-6683 verifies the exact local source row and changes its local publish state; it receives no replacement identity and emits no creator-visible record.
7. `examples/v3-room/src/index.ts:2796-2977` knows the source operation, application displacement policy, semantic identity, and when replacement issues finish. That knowledge remains room-local. The Node close owner never receives it. Expire, manual-review, already-present, rebase, transform, and batched/multiple replacement outcomes therefore cannot be reconstructed generically from the close graph.
8. `packages/issuance-store/src/types.ts:25-89` persists only the signed issued vertex, outbox publish state, and monotonic lineage. There is no settlement/disposition record or proof query. Sequence reset/reuse is not available.
9. `packages/node/src/internal/closed-epoch-cleanup.ts:353-434` can prune a dense locally published prefix after authenticated close/adoption/availability facts, but that local receipt is neither authored by the remote writer for the creator nor carried into the next aggregate. Pruning therefore cannot fill another peer's close frontier.
10. The protocol-v3 vertex registry fixes the signed vertex fields to kind, protocol major, object, epoch, anchor, author, never-resetting author sequence, logical time, dependencies, and application operation. A source/replacement binding is not an existing registered field. Dependencies require active graph meaning and cannot silently be repurposed as old-anchor settlement links.
11. The existing RFC 9162 compact history root authenticates ordered close-set leaves and stays age-independent as compact peaks, but current code neither inserts settlement statements as leaves nor delivers inclusion proofs for them. A history root alone cannot infer which missing source slot a replacement settles.

## Genuine reachability conclusions

- **Rebase trigger:** genuine and already source-traced. Old local issuance is never reset; replacements necessarily allocate later sequence numbers.
- **Delivery-order trigger:** genuine at the admission/close level. Admission does not require author-sequence adjacency, while close does. Once adoption changes the anchor, the missing old-anchor row cannot repair the successor graph.
- **Genesis foreign gap:** reachable in the existing f5a fixture through a genuine signed/admitted foreign sequence greater than one. It remains fail closed at `D110C_0C1F1_LEGACY_MULTI_AUTHOR_MIGRATION_REQUIRED` and is not solved by f5a.
- **Remove/re-add author:** structurally reachable if the existing ACL lifecycle reauthorizes an author omitted from an intervening successor aggregate. The exact product path has not yet been retained as a causal test, so f5b must prove it rather than fabricate aggregate bytes.
- **Shared `(author, sequence)` after foreign regression:** a signed current-epoch row can reuse a sequence numerically covered by prior history. F5a safely refuses to advance; f5b must specify substitution handling before changing historical classification.

## Preferred-family proof obligations

The new prerequisite must freeze one exact construction before RED:

1. A canonical author-signed receipt binds object, pinned genesis, source anchor/epoch/author/sequence/digest, disposition kind, and the exact ordered replacement identities or a zero-replacement terminal outcome. Cross-object, cross-author, cross-epoch, stale-anchor, duplicate-source, duplicate-replacement, partial-batch, reordered-batch, replay, and equivocation inputs fail closed.
2. Manual-review/held rows emit no terminal receipt and prevent settlement-prefix advancement. Expire and already-present outcomes need explicit semantics; neither may be inferred from mutable local publish state alone.
3. The creator accepts a receipt only through authenticated bounded ingress and only when every referenced replacement required by the outcome is genuinely admitted under the intended successor/current authority. Receipt presence alone cannot make application state valid.
4. Each creator close derives the next per-author settled prefix from the prior authenticated checkpoint plus a complete, unique set of admitted current vertices and verified receipts. It may not skip an unaccounted issuance slot or use an observed maximum.
5. The creator-signed checkpoint is bound to object/genesis, closed and successor epoch/anchor, current/successor ACL, cut digest, commit QC, snapshot manifest, history root/size, predecessor checkpoint digest, and a bounded sorted writer vector. The existing v1 admitted frontier can migrate only under an exact reviewed rule; its meaning is not silently rewritten.
6. Ordinary recovery treats rows at or below an authenticated settled frontier as terminal: they are neither republished nor rebased. Rows above it retain current displaced/rebase behavior. A substituted old row cannot affect application state or advance trust.
7. Receipt/control bytes remain until the replacement checkpoint is durably staged, verified, adopted, and protected by the existing rollback/availability policy. Only then may active receipts and published issuance rows be pruned. Archive copies and RFC 9162 proofs are historical evidence, not required active control state for ordinary reopen.
8. Crash ordering covers receipt-before-replacement, replacement-before-receipt, close-before-source-local completion, source completion before close, staged checkpoint before adoption, adoption before pruning, and pruning before restart. Every incomplete ordering either resumes idempotently or remains visibly fail closed.
9. Active control state is bounded independently of epoch count and settled operation count: one current checkpoint, fixed rollback generations, at most 64 writer entries, compact history peaks, and only bounded in-flight receipts for the current transition. If the design cannot bound in-flight receipts without weakening manual-review/offline continuity, it must stop and reslice that resource contract explicitly.
10. The same construction must resolve or explicitly separate genesis first-observed gaps, remove/re-add reachability, no-rebase delivery gaps, rebased gaps, batched replacements, and the shared-sequence regression case.

## Required reslice

The repository does not contain an existing carrier capable of meeting those obligations. The preferred family requires a new authenticated control record and changes to protocol-v3 schema/compatibility, Node ingress/close/recovery, durable custody, and the room handoff API. Those are explicitly prohibited from being folded into f5b without review.

Create **D.110c-0c1f5b0 — author settlement receipt and settled-frontier carrier prerequisite** before f5b RED. Its first deliverable is an exact byte/schema/API/crash/migration design and high-risk Grok/Kimi/Opus review. Production edits remain unauthorized until that review accepts one exact construction with an empty P0/P1 union. If the review cannot establish a bounded in-flight receipt policy or compatibility path, stop at design rather than weakening cold reopen, offline continuity, or durable-census requirements.
