# Replay lifetime and retirement-intent adjuncts

Replay retains authority only while its original registration and native source remain valid. A transport handoff retires the old registration immediately, while physical reads and sends keep their original owners until completion. These Quint projections check that distinction, the bounded resources it protects, and the progress properties that need scheduling assumptions.

The reproducible entry point is [`check-replay-custody.mjs`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/check-replay-custody.mjs). The archived manifest and compressed raw logs under [`evidence/replay-custody`](evidence/replay-custody) identify the exact model, runner, and inspected implementation hashes. A negative control succeeds only when the checker returns a counterexample. Timeouts, translation failures, and process errors cannot count as evidence.

The final source-hashed run passed all 59 checks with unchanged inputs: nine typechecks, ten test modules containing 74 test declarations, 31 TLC checks, and nine Apalache checks. TLC completed the positive graphs and produced all 19 expected safety, temporal, and nonvacuity counterexamples. Apalache passed the eight base/preservation obligations and produced the expected weak-source-age-hypothesis counterexample. These are the scoped properties below, not full-contract induction.

## Authority follows the original owner

[`replay-read-custody.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/replay-read-custody.qnt) contains two projections. The read-phase model has two registrations, two distinct transition attempts, one queued/native read, and a two-row retained prefix. Entering a close or successor-read pause excludes new native reads before source acquisition. Only the matching attempt may complete that transition. A retired registration cannot be restored by a late completion, and a stale prefix rollback cannot restore rows cleared during handoff. A read that outlives its registration cannot report success after releasing native custody.

The borrowed-custody model has one original parent and two independently releasable children. Child cancellation releases only its reference. Parent revocation makes later child acquisition or read checks refuse, while the original native release waits for every outstanding child. The invariant equates reference count to physically held child references plus the still-owned parent reference. Apalache base and one-step obligations start the step proof from every finite state satisfying that law, rather than only reachable test states.

The implementation correspondence is `V3RetainedReplayReadPhase`, `beginRetainedReplayTransition`, `retireRetainedReplay`, `prepareRetainedReplay`, `republishRetained`, and `withCurrentRetainedRead` in [`v3-live.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/v3-live.ts); `borrowJournalHeaderCustody` in [`creator-close.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/creator-close.ts); and `borrowJournalHeaderHold` and the original source/promotion reservation owners in [`creator-closed-rollback-data.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/internal/creator-closed-rollback-data.ts). The model abstracts authentication, exact native snapshot comparisons, FIFO exclusion, cryptographic source binding, and physical release completion. Native tests and implementation review must establish those premises.

## Physical work remains charged across handoffs

[`replay-egress-pool.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/replay-egress-pool.qnt) models a pool owned by a network instance and stable room topic. A job reserves its slot before starting its stream. Its encoded message bytes remain charged until the native send joins, including after cancellation or epoch retirement. The stream slot releases only in its finalizer. The small exhaustive instance has three distinct jobs, three registration generations, a two-slot limit, and three byte units. A separate deterministic instance fills the actual 32-slot shape with two held predecessor sends and thirty current sends; the next admission refuses until a predecessor physically joins.

Each empty-to-nonempty transition creates a new idle generation. Shutdown rechecks the current pool after waking, so an old resolved promise cannot establish that newly admitted work is idle. The temporal property assumes quiescence during the observed shutdown and weak fairness of native completion, finalization, wakeup, and recheck. It proves that a waiting shutdown completes under those premises. It does not promise termination while replacements continue admitting work forever.

This maps to `V3EgressAdmissionOwner`, `reserveEgressAdmission`, `releaseEgressAdmission`, `joinEgressAdmissionOwner`, and the registration's joined-work finalizers in `v3-live.ts`. Native send completion is an explicit environment action. A hung send can therefore keep bytes charged and shutdown pending; cancellation does not manufacture capacity.

## Each broadcast caller has a finite round

[`replay-broadcast-rounds.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/replay-broadcast-rounds.qnt) has two observed callers, a fixed authenticated two-row prefix, and anonymous later demand that can recur indefinitely. A late caller joins the pending full round because it may have missed a row already sent to the current receivers. Finishing the current round resolves its callers before promoting the pending round. With weak fairness of send start, native completion, round finish, and abort, every requested caller eventually completes even when later demand continues forever. Cancellation may complete it with failure; successful completion requires receipt of the full prefix.

The safety mutation attaches a late caller to the current round and produces an incomplete-prefix success. The temporal mutation waits for demand to become quiet and produces a fair infinite execution that never completes an earlier caller. This is the distinction implemented by the separate current/pending results in `V3ReplayableEgressDemand` and `enqueueRetainedPublication`.

## A floor does not identify a retirement intent

[`retirement-intent-selection.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/retirement-intent-selection.qnt) holds the through-epoch fixed and varies the decision and authenticated source identities independently. An exact active retry preserves its original holes and rejects a foreign intent. Physical journal deletion during recovery does not itself invalidate the old authenticated identity: reconstruction may still establish the original source. After completion, a fresh same-floor intent derives holes from the current eligible rows, excludes previously retired rows, and keeps a replacement while its source remains live. Reusing completed holes by floor alone fails the model's selection law.

The three-row projection contains one source/replacement dependency and one independent row. Publication, replay-scope eligibility, authenticated label derivation, and the shared transitive dependency closure are premises. This is a selection law, not a proof that every maintenance episode creates enough room for another application edit. It applies to the shared `closedIssuanceProtectedRecordsForRetirement` intent distinction. Legacy creator-trusted operation intentionally retains its issuance history; this model adds no legacy deletion policy or indefinite legacy progress claim.

The implementation hashes in this replay manifest identify the replay baseline, before the companion exact-intent selection fix. That baseline's same-floor reuse is the negative control's behavior. A separately identified [companion snapshot](evidence/replay-custody/companion-implementation.json) records the inspected changed source hashes and exact selection-function excerpt; it was uncommitted when captured. The positive selection model must be composed with the companion fix and its native recovery tests; this manifest alone is not evidence that the baseline implementation satisfies it.

[`retirement-recovery-admission.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/retirement-recovery-admission.qnt) makes the admission gate explicit. Only the original private request with an exact current head, matching preparation domains, the current predecessor floor, and no nominee may enter interrupted-intent reconciliation. Those authenticated conditions are abstracted by the qualification predicate. Ordinary admission stays halted through durable completion, nonempty proof verification, and the native release join. A crash clears runtime verification; reopening must establish it again. The model rejects opening on the completed control alone, accepting a foreign active request, or treating an empty proof as terminal evidence.

## Counter currency preserves an already authenticated quote

[`issuance-quote-currency.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/issuance-quote-currency.qnt) covers the ordinary no-plan/no-effect optimization. Its starting premise is a full authenticated image, control, lineage, and capacity capture. The first owned phase accounts for an optional control birth and a missing reserved marker plus revision bump; the second accounts for a transition to entered plus its revision bump. Existing markers contribute zero own changes. At each COMMIT/BEGIN boundary, foreign `data_version`, same-connection `total_changes`, exact control, owned pending lineage, and incarnation must still match the retained postimage before crypto may start.

The finite model explores both gaps, optional control birth, fresh/reserved/entered starting markers, up to two unexpected mutations, and unexpected own-DML count. Rebaselining to the changed counters and skipping a recheck produce counterexamples. It assumes cooperative native writers, stable counter semantics without wrap, exclusion under `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, a private connection, and rejection of mutating triggers. It does not prove those SQLite guarantees, count canonical encoded bytes, or replace the full reread path for plan effects. The companion native mutation controls are the evidence for actual connection behavior.

## Current-fence proof boundary

[`current-fence-projection.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/current-fence-projection.qnt) retains only one writer's epoch, first sequence, next sequence, and row epoch/kind. Its relation permits a frame step, an append at the next sequence, or a new fence followed by replacement rows. Apalache checks the base and preservation obligation across all states in the declared finite domains satisfying the current-fence law. This avoids the unrelated maps that made the earlier full-state query expensive.

[`source-age-projection.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/source-age-projection.qnt) separately checks the source-age law. Its supporting invariant bounds every issued row's epoch by the installed epoch. Install/readmission advances the epoch before assigning dispositions to previously issued sources, giving `source.epoch <= oldEpoch < targetEpoch`; append does not assign a disposition, and manual resolution changes an existing disposition's value. The stronger hypothesis gets its own base and one-step checks. The weak-hypothesis negative control omits the issued-row bound, allowing an impossible future-epoch source to expose why source age alone is insufficient for induction.

[`current-fence-bridge.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/current-fence-bridge.qnt) observes actual Track J transitions and checks that their projected before/after pair satisfies both relations, along with current fence, source age, and the issued-row epoch bound. The instance uses the previous one-writer, adjacent-install, expire-readmission configuration with maximum epoch 3 and sequence 4. It does not model the new public arbitrary-gap/manual-preserve path.

The source mapping is narrow: `move` and replacement-producing `resolveRows` append; successful `installToAt` and `freshState` write the new fence; expiration, failed installation, seal, leave, prune, and recovery cleanup frame these fields. The reachable-state bridge is useful evidence for that mapping, but is not an arbitrary-state refinement proof. Full Track J contract induction remains open: the actual source-age lemma and current-fence refinement must be established for the complete induction hypothesis before either fact can be added as a proved premise. Finite exhaustive graphs and the small projected induction do not establish safety or progress for arbitrary writer/row/epoch bounds.

The prior undecided full-state fence-preservation and fenced-contract-move queries remain archived in [`evidence/native-custody`](evidence/native-custody), alongside their source hashes and the failed contract hypotheses described in [NATIVE-CUSTODY.md](NATIVE-CUSTODY.md#contract-induction-remains-open). This adjunct does not change those verdicts. It supplies separately scoped obligations rather than converting a finite bridge or an undecided full-state query into a full-contract theorem.

Independent review first identified that the newly added recovery and currency models were missing from the complete runner. Their typechecks, tests, safety checks, counterexamples, nonvacuity checks, and hashes were registered before the final run. A second independent review reran all 59 checks and reported no actionable correctness issues. Both raw reviews are archived with the evidence.

## Reproduce

With the repository's installed Quint 0.32 toolchain and Java 17:

```sh
node packages/protocol-v3/formal/check-replay-custody.mjs all
```

`REPLAY_LOGS` selects the evidence directory; `REPLAY_ENDPOINT` selects an unused local Apalache server endpoint; `TRACK_J_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` sets the per-check bound (300 seconds by default). The runner owns each checker's process group, including its JVM descendants, and retires that group after completion or timeout. The `test`, `verify`, and `induction` modes permit targeted reruns without treating a partial mode as the complete suite.
