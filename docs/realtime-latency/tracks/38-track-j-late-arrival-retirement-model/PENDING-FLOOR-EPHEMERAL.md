# Installed ephemeral authority across native floor transitions

An installed ephemeral context and a durable room floor answer different questions. E2 identifies the exact live object, epoch, anchor, and ACL under which a frame can be delivered. The floor controls fresh activation and native publication currency. A pending floor forbids activating either candidate; it does not, by itself, prove that an already installed old E2 context must disappear. The floor's eventual retirement of the old stable tuple is also not an explicit E2 deactivation rule.

This distinction follows the existing contracts in [D.93.48 and D.110c-0b0](../../../production-hardening/production-hardening-tdd-plan-v2.md): the former makes the installed live owner authoritative for E2 and revokes its view on deactivation; the latter specifies pending recovery and exact dual-owner equality before new activation. This adjunct therefore permits old E2 traffic while the old registration and authority metadata remain installed, including the native commit-to-registration interval. It does not claim that such traffic is a shipped defect. A policy requiring every E2 callback to match the latest durable floor would be a stronger product decision.

## Host turns do not transfer authority

The lifecycle in [`pending-floor-ephemeral.qnt`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/pending-floor-ephemeral.qnt) separates each native begin, head publication, and floor commit from observation of its promise result. Host callbacks may run between those actions. Neither a Node microtask train nor the presence of a held native writer is modeled as host-turn exclusion. The native owner still excludes conflicting native mutation and authenticates the exact selected transition; that exclusion does not suspend timers, network callbacks, or the installed E2 context.

During replacement the old room handle can remain visible after the registration map has changed. Its live authority method must then return unavailable. Once the room binds its replacement handle and authority metadata, only that replacement's exact tuple is accepted. A cached public projection record is not a substitute for this live authority check. The model preserves the temporary gap instead of manufacturing old authority or prematurely exposing the new one.

The inspected hot handoff in `v3-live.ts` synchronously marks the old registration inactive and replaces the topic-map entry. Later, `performCreatorSuccessorAdoption` in `v3-room.ts` assigns `activeHandle` and `successorProjectionAuthority` together, installs the authenticated projection base synchronously, then awaits `commitSuccessorProjection`. The model's `authorityBinding` is this authority metadata, not completed application projection replay. If replay awaits `onAcceptedVertex`, application projection callbacks can lag new E2 exposure. Their state/roster composition is a separate implementation obligation; the model does not assert simultaneous application-view publication.

An encoded old frame can remain in a native send or queue after replacement. Finishing that send is not fresh authorization. The receiver reads its current authority and rejects the old tuple. The old-byte rejection claim here is scoped to a changed authenticated tuple in the modeled adjacent transition. Registration identity is local provider liveness, not an identifier in wire bytes. A same-epoch transport replacement can preserve a valid authenticated tuple. Equality includes the anchor and ACL as well as epoch; the model has a foreign tuple at the same numeric epoch to exercise this distinction. Network sender authentication and membership are premises, not modeled cryptography.

The inspected receive callback in [`packages/ephemeral`](../../../../packages/ephemeral/src/index.ts) has no await between its authority read, writer check, exact frame decode, sequence check, and first subscriber delivery. Yields in the native transition do not split that segment. A hypothetical new await inside receive would need registration and authority revalidation before delivery; the separate owner projection includes that negative control. Subscriber reentrancy, asynchronous application continuations, and application-owned buffering are outside this atomic segment and outside the proof.

## Suspension is a separate policy

The same lifecycle also checks an optional conservative policy that suspends E2 before requesting begin and restores it only after exact new floor, registration, handle, and authority metadata agree. That policy eliminates pending and post-commit old-context traffic, at an availability cost. Its passing graph does not recommend adding such a gate or establish a missing requirement in the installed-authority contract.

If a gate is chosen, suspension must cover native effects before their promise observations. An ambiguous rejection cannot restore old authority: only independently established no-effect can abort before pending installation. After pending, cold recovery completes the same selected transition or remains unavailable. Existing old-view traffic and cold activation of an old generation are deliberately different actions. The latter is forbidden while pending in both policies.

The owner projection separates local attempt identity from local registration identity. Its original-continuation policy rechecks the captured registration after a hypothetical await; a different design could reacquire and validate the new provider with an unchanged wire tuple. No on-wire registration identifier is assumed. Actual completion and delivery decisions retain their pre-state authorization evidence, so weakening a decision guard is detected independently of a mutant flag. An obsolete completion cannot release a later attempt's gate, restore a changed registration, or undo close. Its finite arbitrary-state base and one-step induction are scoped to those fields under the stated invariant; they do not establish arbitrary integer epochs, the full floor lifecycle, native exclusion, or TypeScript refinement. The projection supplies conditional obligations for any proposed gate and for a future split receive callback; it does not imply either exists in production.

## Implementation correspondence and evidence

| Model boundary | Existing owner | Required correspondence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native effect followed by promise observation | Room-head begin/commit and successor publication in [`v3-room.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/v3-room.ts) | Keep exact original native owner and selected pending identity across yields; no fresh activation while pending |
| Installed context becomes unavailable on replacement | `currentRegistration` and `makeV3PlaneHandle` in [`v3-live.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/v3-live.ts) | Check active flag, exact registration-map identity, and topic membership on every authority read |
| Room binds live authority to E2 | `openEphemeral` in [`v3-room.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/v3-room.ts) | Use the active handle's live provider, not the retained `authority()` projection record |
| Old encoded sends and fresh receives | Queue drain and receive callback in [`packages/ephemeral`](../../../../packages/ephemeral/src/index.ts) | Preserve encoded tuple; perform fresh exact receiver comparison; retain the synchronous pre-delivery segment |
| Cold new activation | [`creator-adoption-activate.ts`](../../../../packages/node/src/creator-adoption-activate.ts) and original recovery owners | Authenticate exact native head/floor agreement before installing a replacement |

The source-hashed registry is [`check-pending-floor.mjs`](../../../../packages/protocol-v3/formal/check-pending-floor.mjs). [Archived evidence](evidence/pending-floor) distinguishes early new activation, cold old activation, stale provider/queue/tuple mutants from observational counterexamples to the stronger suspension policy. Timeout, translation failure, or non-verdict exit cannot satisfy an expected counterexample. The source snapshot identifies the inspected baseline; no model result is a proof that a later implementation refines these actions.

These small graphs support host interleavings around already awaitable owner boundaries, provided the correspondence premises hold. They do not prove a latency bound, scheduler fairness, durable-operation admission during transitions, WebRTC behavior, native crash persistence, or the still-open full-contract induction. The earlier rejoin and replay model sources and their exact-hash evidence are unchanged.

With installed Quint, Apalache, and Java 17, run `node packages/protocol-v3/formal/check-pending-floor.mjs all`. The registry also accepts `test`, `verify`, and `induction`; `PENDING_FLOOR_LOGS`, `PENDING_FLOOR_ENDPOINT`, and `PENDING_FLOOR_CASE_FILTER` select evidence location, checker endpoint, and an explicitly partial rerun. The existing watchdog owns and reaps every checker process group.

Independent review identified and fixed two model assertion gaps: queued delivery now contributes traffic observations, and grant/delivery invariants use recorded authorization evidence instead of a mutant failure flag. The [final review](evidence/pending-floor/independent-review.json) found no actionable defects and separately records its reproduced checks. The superseded inputs and first review remain archived for provenance.
